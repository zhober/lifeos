# Nutrition and photographed-meal contract

Read this contract for meal photographs, nutrition calculations, dietary assessment, and nutrition targets. The model/runtime interprets images and context; Node utilities calculate and preserve ordinary files. The main assistant writes records. The nutritionist remains read-only. Follow AGENTS.md, docs/core/HANDBOOK.md, docs/core/MEMORY.md, docs/core/CONTROL.md, and docs/core/REVIEW.md.

Text and photo meals, and corrections of a saved meal (`supersedes`), take the quick path in `workflows/log/QUICK.md`; quick-log preserves photo originals and produces canonical sourced meal records, so the rules below apply unchanged. The calling model views the images and supplies the components.

## Meal photos as the normal input

The user normally photographs each meal. A clear photo is sufficient to start an estimated record; do not require ingredient lists or weighing for every meal. When useful, suggest a view showing the whole plate, a familiar size reference, or an additional side/leftover/label photo. These are optional aids, not mandatory onboarding steps.

Preserve the supplied original bytes through `tools/nutrition.mjs photo`. Originals live in `inbox/meal-photos/`; camera metadata does not establish the meal date without context. Use the user's stated date, otherwise today's date in the configured timezone (`node tools/settings.mjs show`) with the assumption recorded. Do not modify originals, generate replacement photos, or publish images for a search.

For an attachment-only request, preserve its images first, then create the durable request with `original_input: ""` and nonempty `attachments` referencing the retained originals. Do not fabricate a spoken user instruction. Mixed requests retain the exact supplied text and their attachment references. Attachment traces and model observations remain distinct from verbatim user statements.

Image tasks require actual image understanding: open the retained image using the available viewing tool or supply its bytes through the runtime. Text-reading a filename does not inspect its contents. If vision is unavailable, retain the original and a pending meal observation with `image_review: unavailable`, no fabricated image components, and unknown nutrition. Text/label information supplied separately can still support an explicitly text-based or mixed estimate. A later capable runtime creates a sourced revision.

Group multiple views, duplicates, packaging, and before/after images of one eating occasion under one meal ID. Re-uploading the same meal is not another eating event; a separate occasion gets another ID even if the image bytes are identical. Before photos describe food served; actual intake also considers leftovers, sharing, added portions, drinks, snacks, oils, and sauces. A single personal-plate photo may provisionally assume full consumption, clearly labeled; shared dishes or important leftovers require a portion estimate or focused clarification. Do not silently assume an entire shared dish was eaten by one person.

## Visual interpretation and quantification

For each component retain its name, visible observations, candidate identity, cooking state, edible-weight basis, portion method, grams, consumed fraction, source references, and separate qualitative identity/portion/recipe confidence. Keep observed features separate from inferred ingredients or quantities. Use explicit low/estimate/high bounds for photo or recipe estimates; do not invent calibrated probability percentages. These are assumption/sensitivity ranges, not statistical confidence intervals.

Use high-confidence food identity when justified by the image/context. If several identities materially change the estimate, resolve them with at most one or two decisive questions or leave the component unquantified. The calculator treats low/unknown identity, unknown quantity/intake, unknown preparation basis, and unconfirmed database matches as unknown. A numeric point cannot replace a missing input.

Visible dish area alone does not establish weight or hidden ingredients. A missing scale can be handled with an explicitly assumed household portion and a broad range when reasonable. Record the assumption and low portion confidence. Prefer the user's measured quantity, relevant label, calibrated familiar container, or established personal recipe when available; retain method and date.

Choose a comparable food by exact database code/name, checking raw/cooked form and per-100-g edible portion. `basis_match` records confirmed, assumed, or unknown suitability. Keep explicit assumptions for a proxy. For a mixed dish use either a comparable prepared-dish entry or a non-overlapping ingredient model; do not count both the complete dish and its ingredients, or add oil already included in its reference recipe.

For a readable package label, a component may provide `composition` with source_kind `package_label`, basis `per_100g`, `per_100ml`, or `per_serving`, original nutrient values/units, and retained sources. Each value uses `{value, unit}`; unavailable values are null. Use grams, milliliters, or servings respectively, plus the consumed fraction. The utility converts compatible g/mg/ug and kJ/kcal units and retains the original label. Do not equate milliliters with grams without evidence or confuse a whole package with one labeled serving. Unclear OCR, basis, or unit remains unknown until clarified.

A label value may also specify qualitative `confidence`; low or unknown reading confidence leaves the calculated nutrient unknown even when a provisional number is retained in the original observation.

Hidden oil, sugar, sauces, and salt need recipe/label context or explicit ingredient assumptions. Include separately modeled ingredients with bounded quantities, or an unknown component when their contribution cannot be defensibly estimated. Do not infer exact sodium, nutrient deficiency, cooking loss, or vitamin content from appearance alone. Micronutrient estimates are reference-based proxies subject to recipe and preparation uncertainty; omit unsupported values rather than fill every nutrient.

Uncertainty is nutrient-specific. An optional component `structural_zeros` maps a supported nutrient key to `{reason, sources}`. Use it only for a justified structural absence of that nutrient in a high-confidence identified food with confirmed preparation/basis and a zero reference value. Evidence must go beyond the food-table/label zero and the meal photograph; retain a separate durable explanation/source, preferably with a quotation establishing its applicability. A rounded zero, unreadable label, uncertain identity, or unknown recipe does not establish structural absence. The calculator validates the metadata and saves source hashes; the model must assess the reasoning. For a supported structural zero, unknown quantity or consumption does not affect that nutrient's modeled contribution. All other unavailable contributions remain null. A meal can retain usable energy/protein while its sodium remains unknown, with quality `partially_quantified` rather than a claim that all quantities were reported.

## Calculation and durable files

```text
node tools/nutrition.mjs photo <input.json>       Preserve a user-supplied image
node tools/nutrition.mjs calculate <input.json>   Calculate model-supplied components
node tools/nutrition.mjs record <input.json>      Save an immutable meal version
node tools/nutrition.mjs coverage <input.json>    Save a sourced full-day coverage statement
node tools/nutrition.mjs independent <input.json> Save an independent daily nutrient claim
node tools/nutrition.mjs meals [date]             Read current meal versions
node tools/nutrition.mjs needs <input.json>       Calculate a screened adult energy estimate
node tools/food.mjs search <food> --json          Find reference candidates
node tools/food.mjs calc "<exact-food> <grams>" --json
```

Use `templates/meal.json`. Input includes stable `operation_id`, date, meal_name, input_method, image_review, observation, photos, assumptions, components, and durable sources. Each component has id, name, food_key, cooking_state, basis_match, visibility, portion_method, grams, consumed_fraction, consumption_basis, confidence, assumptions, and sources. Known intervals use `low`, `estimate`, `high`; unknown intervals are null. Photo/recipe quantities require a nonzero range and explanation. A sourced report of zero consumption is genuinely zero, unlike an unknown food or amount.

`record` saves `records/nutrition/meals/YYYY/<meal-id>/versions/*.json`, including exact observations, image references, calculation output, food composition snapshots, and source hashes. Use the same meal_id and supersede the exact latest version for a correction, additional photo, leftover estimate, or changed date. Retraction preserves earlier versions. Identical operation retries are idempotent; changed payloads use new operations. Do not edit derived values in an existing version. Data-file upgrades do not retroactively replace the captured composition used for old estimates.

The retained textual description, assumptions, units, and results let text-only models read prior analysis; original images remain available for later visual reassessment. Unavailable nutrients stay null. The current validated-unit set includes energy, protein, fat, carbohydrate, fiber, sodium, calcium, iron, potassium, magnesium, zinc, vitamin C, B1, B2, and niacin. Other nutrients may be added after verifying source units and values. Food-table zeros/reference values are not measurements of the particular photographed meal. Food-water composition is separate from recorded drinking water.

The record command returns `journal_projection` with the existing table format and its meal ID/source link. Use it once for an active meal and update that same organized row on correction; remove or mark a retracted projection while retaining raw history. Meal times are recorded only when supplied; capture time is not silently substituted for eating time.

A retry of a superseded capture returns its original saved result with the current-version link and no projection; do not reapply stale display text after a later correction. Conflicting version branches require source-preserving reconciliation instead of choosing an arbitrary winner.

Preserve original statements and journal headings/table columns. Add one projected row per meal with `meal:<meal-id>` in its source cell and a link to the canonical version. Display a point only when available, visibly marked as estimated; put ranges/assumptions in the record or adjacent prose. Do not put a range in a scalar cell or copy every component into separate extra rows for the same meal. `day.mjs sum` reads canonical versions, ignores linked projection duplicates/stale versions, and also supports legacy rows.

For independently supplied daily totals, use `templates/nutrition-independent.json` and `independent`. Preserve the original export or exact user statement first. Each nutrient key uses `{value, unit}` with the supported canonical unit; `value` is a number, low/estimate/high interval, or null. Explicit `complete` and `reason` describe whether the claim covers all intake that day. Do not invent full-day scope from an export filename. Numeric/source completeness and clinical suitability are still separate. A captured source can support a reported or estimated total without making it a measurement.

Independent claims are immutable versions under `records/nutrition/totals/YYYY/<date>/versions/`. A correction/retraction supplies a new operation ID and `supersedes` pointing to the exact latest daily version. Retraction can use empty nutrients, retaining its reason/sources. A date correction retracts the old date and creates a new claim on the correct date. Identical retries return the same version, even after aggregation or a later revision; the capture command does not rewrite the journal. Run `day.mjs sum` after capture to refresh the display. Retries never reapply a superseded display value. This avoids regenerating a hash-checked write request under an already used operation ID.

An independent value replaces the corresponding display slot; it is never added to the meals. Other nutrients continue from their own evidence. Preserve both meal-derived and independent claims. Compare for disagreement only when both describe a complete day and both provide that nutrient; disjoint sensitivity intervals indicate a conflict, using a 0.01 rounding tolerance in the canonical unit. Overlapping intervals do not prove agreement or accuracy. Partial meal logs and an independently supplied whole-day total can coexist. A partial independent claim is readable but cannot supply a complete-day comparison. A contradiction with an explicit zero-intake statement remains a conflict.

## Completeness and statistics

Every nutrient reports a meal-derived total only when all recorded components have that value; otherwise it reports null plus a separate known subtotal and coverage. Unknown items are not zero. Missing nutrients do not acquire false completeness merely because energy is known. Distinguish three axes: `coverage` describes numeric availability, `intake_day_status` describes whole-day/event scope, and `nutrient_quality` describes source traceability. `estimate_qualities` retains portion/recipe uncertainty from the meals. Traceable estimates are still estimates. Daily frontmatter retains each nutrient's known subtotal, range, status, quality, origin, and comparison eligibility. Legacy table rows and frontmatter totals with no canonical provenance remain `legacy_unverified` or `mixed_unverified`, even if a source cell contains free-form text or someone confirms that all eating events were recorded. `nutrition_state: partial` may coexist with unverified provenance.

To verify a legacy row, retain its original evidence/history and create a sourced canonical meal version with quantities, reference/basis, assumptions, and actual consumption. Replace only the organized row with its canonical projection, then renew the day confirmation if appropriate. Use an independent claim for genuinely supplied daily totals; do not invent ingredient evidence to upgrade an old number.

Complete recorded items are not necessarily a complete day. Only an explicit sourced statement that all meals, drinks, snacks, and additions were included can confirm full-day coverage, for example "everything I ate today is recorded." Do not infer it from three photos or the user's intention to photograph meals. Use `templates/meal-day-coverage.json`. Coverage is bound to current meal versions and legacy rows; changes invalidate it until reconciled. Explicit reported zero intake is separately recorded and cannot overwrite conflicting meal evidence.

Source hashes are checked for meal/component evidence, label evidence, structural-zero proofs, independent totals, and coverage statements. Missing or changed evidence remains unverified or invalidates the relevant coverage confirmation. Prefer immutable inbox originals or original request/message artifacts as sources. Referencing an actively edited journal by whole-file hash will become stale when that journal changes; preserve a dated source artifact before deriving from it. Updating the global food database does not invalidate a previously captured composition snapshot. Source traceability is not evidence of biological accuracy.

Conflicts return structured claims and flags rather than throwing for an ordinary contradictory nutrition report. Affected totals/ranges are null and excluded from comparison; observed known subtotals, `recorded_claim`, `independent_claim`, and the coverage record remain available for reconciliation. A zero-intake contradiction can affect the whole day's nutrition, including zero-energy foods with nonzero nutrients or unknown actual consumption. Work, study, and other metrics remain readable. Resolve through sourced revisions; do not hide a conflict by rerunning aggregation. Invalid file schemas or branched versions still require repair instead of an arbitrary winner.

`day` and `stats` use the same live resolver. A canonical meal link, a per-field origin, or the historical `nutrition_derived` marker can identify a cache. Moved, retracted, or missing linked records invalidate their projected cache even before the first successful sum. Independent claim versions survive meal corrections; independent retraction invalidates its own cache. Explicit old `<field>_origin: independent` values are preserved as `independent_unverified` until reconstructed through a sourced claim. Values with no origin or link remain ambiguous legacy data rather than being guessed away. Displaced values are reported under `reconciliation`, retained in `<field>_previous_*` when applicable, and preserved in checked-write before/after history. A legacy total disputed by a zero-intake statement also retains `<field>_legacy_claim`; rerunning a sum does not erase that unresolved evidence.

Statistics expose partial/unconfirmed/legacy/conflict days, available sample counts, known subtotals, and ranges. The existing `avg` remains the mean of available recorded totals. For each nutrient with numeric observations, `complete_day_n`, `complete_day_avg`, `complete_day_range`, and `complete_day_dates` use only that nutrient's eligible complete days. Eligibility requires a numeric value, sourced full-day scope, traceable nutrient evidence, and no applicable conflict; unknown sodium does not exclude otherwise supported protein. The mean interval averages the included sensitivity bounds; it is not a statistical confidence interval. An empty eligible sample has count zero and null mean/range; a nutrient with no numeric observations has no summary entry. Use the complete-day statistic and its actual dates/sample size when comparing with applicable targets. A generic available-record mean cannot establish full daily intake. Missing logs are not nonadherence. Do not conclude low intake or nutrient deficiency from an incomplete record. Keep raw records and older estimates when correcting a row or importing new evidence.

## Individual goals and feedback

For shared health/nutrition questions use docs/core/COLLABORATION.md and the same evidence-linked case as the health advisor, with clinical source context under docs/domains/HEALTH.md. Return nutrient/coverage uncertainty, supporting/contrary observations, and scoped questions through the main assistant. Dietary estimates do not verify a clinical explanation, and a clinical source does not supply missing meal quantities. Reassess after source revisions.

For advice read profile/goals, `records/nutrition/preferences.md`, current targets and prior review history, relevant full-history memories, source meal records, and prior advice/action outcomes. Meal-only image transcription does not require a full lifestyle consultation; use relevant identity/recipe restrictions and sufficient context, then return the structured observation for calculation.

Establish the user's actual goal and practical preferences without repeatedly asking answered questions. Identify age/life stage, applicable health restrictions, current measurements, and activity assumptions before choosing reference targets. Use population-appropriate authoritative guidance and verify updates under shared browsing rules. Do not apply one protein interval or a weight-loss goal universally, infer allergies/disease from a photo, or automatically prescribe supplements or condition-specific diets. Coordinate with the health advisor and appropriate professional care when the question requires it.

The needs utility implements the simplified Mifflin-St Jeor resting-energy formula for a confirmed general adult, with supplied equation coefficients/measurements and explicit activity-factor bounds. It does not guess missing age/sex, default an activity factor, create a calorie deficit, or set a protein target. It flags populations outside the applicable screening procedure and the original study age range. Activity-factor bounds do not capture all equation uncertainty. Save the calculation and source assumptions when selecting a proposed target.

Canonical measurements are numeric `age_years`, `height_cm`, and `weight_kg`. Explicit alternatives are `height: {value, unit: "cm"|"m"}` and `weight: {value, unit: "kg"|"g"}`; only declared units are converted. Conflicting canonical/explicit measurements, unknown units, invalid types, and values outside shared `nutrition.input_sanity_bounds` prevent an applicable energy estimate. Invalid dimensions leave BMI null. For example, `height_cm: 1.75` is flagged rather than guessed to mean meters; an explicitly stated 1.75 m becomes 175 cm. Results retain `normalized_inputs`, reasons, and `input_issues`. The shared broad bounds detect common software/unit mistakes; they are not healthy ranges, diagnoses, or a replacement for population-specific assessment. Preserve the supplied input with the calculation artifact.

Use `templates/nutrition-target.json` as the proposed-target structure and preserve dated target versions/history in `records/nutrition/targets.md` or linked immutable task artifacts. Specify goal, applicability, numerical ranges and units, source titles/URLs/lookup dates, validity, review date, and evidence basis. Distinguish proposed from user-accepted targets and connect accepted changes to the shared goals/memory/action history. Material advice follows docs/core/REVIEW.md before release.

Normally revisit a newly adopted target around the shared `nutrition.review_days` period, with a user-appropriate date recorded; this is an operational reminder, not a clinical rule or autonomous timer. Compare suitable periods using scripts, report weight/intake sample counts and completeness, consider actual adherence, preparation/portion changes, hunger/tolerance feedback, and historical ineffective methods. Do not alter a target from one weight value, sparse estimates, or an unverified assumption. Keep it, adjust it with evidence and rationale, or defer judgment while filling decisive gaps. A single relevant event can enter sourced memory immediately; a personal pattern still needs the shared longitudinal evidence.

Store reusable external references in `experts/nutritionist/knowledge.md`, with evidence levels and dates; personal preferences and estimates belong in personal records/memory. Do not translate old user statements. Return concise, neutral, practical adjustments in the configured language, usually at most three, with major assumptions and missing information visible.
